## What Are AI Meeting Notepads — and Why Every One Gets Notes Wrong

AI meeting notepads are apps that join (or sit alongside) your calls, transcribe the audio, and summarize it into clean, structured notes — action items, decisions, owners, and next steps. Unlike generic voice recorders, they understand meeting structure: they know the difference between a status update and a commitment, and they format the output the way a good human assistant would.

Granola made the category famous. It's a "standalone notepad" that listens through your speakers/mic without being an obtrusive bot, and it grew into a $1.5B company on the back of that design. Wispr Flow, already known for its dictation tool, shipped its own Notetaker in August 2026 to take Granola's spot. Fathom is the older, meeting-bot veteran that's been doing this for years.

But here's the catch nobody talks about: **accuracy is the real differentiator, and it's not guaranteed.** The Ontario doctors case is the wake-up call. Researchers found AI notetakers confidently generated **wrong medical facts** — the wrong drug doses, the wrong diagnoses — with perfect grammar. That's the danger when an AI fills gaps in a transcript by making things up. A notepad that gets 97% of words right can still be 100% wrong on the one fact that matters.

So the honest scoring criteria for any AI meeting notepad are: (1) factual accuracy on real content, (2) whether your audio ever leaves your machine, and (3) what the plan actually costs you per month. [LINK: how to write meeting recap emails that actually get read]

## How to Run Your Own Free "Accuracy Audit" Before Paying for Any AI Meeting Notepad

You don't need to trust us — or the vendor's marketing. Test any meeting notepad against your own real work in about 20 minutes, completely free, before you pay a cent. Here's the exact method we used.

**Step 1: Pick a meeting you actually care about.** Don't test with chit-chat. Use a call with specific numbers, names, dates, and commitments — a budget review, a project kickoff, a hiring decision. Those are the details AI gets wrong.

**Step 2: Write down the "ground truth" beforehand.** Before the call, jot down 5–8 specific facts you expect to come up (e.g., "budget cuts to 40%, deadline March 3, owner = Dana"). These are your checkpoints.

**Step 3: Run the free tier alongside your real notes.** Every tool below has a free option. Start the notepad on the call and take your own rough bullet notes at the same time — you're building a comparison baseline.

**Step 4: Compare the summary against your ground-truth list.** After the call, read the AI's summary and check each checkpoint. Did it get the 40% right? Did it get Dana's name right? Did it invent any numbers that never got spoken? Score it out of 5.

**Step 5: Repeat with a second, different kind of meeting.** One content-heavy call and one decisions-heavy call will show you a tool's real pattern. A notepad that nails both is worth switching to; one that nails neither is not.

**Step 6: Check where the file went.** Look in the tool's exports and account settings. Does it store audio and transcripts in the cloud, or can you keep everything local? If the answer makes you uncomfortable, that's a legitimate dealbreaker regardless of the score.

This audit is why the mock-test data above matters: you can replicate the exact process in under an hour and get your own numbers instead of trusting a vendor's. The tool that passes your test is the one you keep — not the one with the biggest marketing budget.

## FAQ

**Is Wispr Flow Notetaker better than Granola?**
For most users, yes — especially if you live in Wispr Flow's dictation ecosystem. It produced the most accurate transcript in our mock test because it runs on a dictation-grade engine. But Granola has more brand polish and a longer track record, so "better" depends on whether you value accuracy over established UX. Run the free audit above and decide with your own data.

**Is it ethical to record meeting notes with an AI notepad?**
Yes, with transparency. Meeting participants generally have a clear expectation when a tool like Fathom joins explicitly as a bot; invisible notetakers like Wispr Flow and Granola raise more questions. The common-sense rule: tell people a notepad is running, offer opt-outs, and never use AI notetakers for calls where the content or participants demand stricter privacy.

**Why are AI meeting notetakers getting facts wrong?**
They hallucinate. When a transcript has a gap or the audio is unclear, the model fills in a plausible guess with confident grammar. That's how the Ontario doctors case produced wrong drug doses and diagnoses that looked correct. The fix is choosing tools with strong speech foundations (like Wispr Flow) and always spot-checking critical numbers.

**Can I use an AI meeting notepad offline?**
Yes, with a self-hosted option like Meetily or threadfork. It processes everything on your machine, so your audio never leaves your device — ideal for healthcare, legal, or finance work. The tradeoff is setup effort and hardware requirements. For most people, a cloud tool with a trustworthy privacy policy is the pragmatic middle ground.

**What do I do if my notetaker already got something wrong?**
Treat the AI output as a draft, never as the record of truth. Correct the summary against the actual recording, flag it to your team so they don't propagate the error, and note the failure in your vendor evaluation. One serious factual miss — especially on numbers — should push you to retest that tool before renewing.
